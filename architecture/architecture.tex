Enigma comprises three Solidity 0.8.20 contracts built on the audited OpenZeppelin v5.1.0 library set~\cite{openzeppelin} --- \texttt{EnigCredit} (ERC-20~\cite{eip20}), \texttt{Marketplace} (listings and escrow), and \texttt{Reputation} --- plus a vanilla JavaScript/ethers.js front end that targets either a local Anvil chain (chainId 31337) or Sepolia (11155111) selected at runtime. Reads use a JSON-RPC provider; state-changing calls are signed through the user's wallet.

\textbf{Security by standards.} Rather than hand-rolling sensitive logic, every primitive reuses an audited OpenZeppelin module, shrinking the custom attack surface and inheriting years of review. Credits are a standard ERC-20 token with EIP-2612 \texttt{permit}, so a buyer authorizes escrow with an off-chain signature instead of a lingering allowance. The threat model assumes mutually distrusting buyers and sellers and a hostile network observer. Key risks and mitigations are: unauthorized credit issuance, prevented by an \texttt{Ownable} owner-only \texttt{mint} (a capped, rate-limited public faucet supplies demo top-ups without granting arbitrary issuance); payment theft or reentrancy during settlement, prevented by holding funds in escrow and applying the checks-effects-interactions pattern together with \texttt{ReentrancyGuard} and \texttt{SafeERC20}; double-rating and reputation forgery, prevented by binding each one-time rating to the verified buyer of a completed sale; and seller non-delivery, mitigated by a timeout-based refund. \texttt{Pausable} provides an emergency stop, while Solidity~0.8.20 checked arithmetic and explicit custom errors harden the contracts further. Correctness is verified in CI by unit, fuzz, and invariant tests alongside Slither static analysis.
