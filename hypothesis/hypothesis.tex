We hypothesize that the core trust functions of a marketplace---value, listings, escrow, and reputation---can be implemented \emph{entirely on-chain} from audited standard components such that: (H1) no party can violate the safety invariants (only the owner mints; escrowed funds are released only to the seller on buyer confirmation or refunded to the buyer; each completed sale yields at most one rating from its verified buyer); (H2) the system withstands the threat model of Section~\ref{sec:threat} without bespoke cryptography; and (H3) per-operation cost and confirmation latency remain within practical bounds for a community-scale testnet deployment. We further hypothesize that decomposing the system into four vertical slices allows independent development and evaluation while composing into one correct system.
