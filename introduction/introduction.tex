Online marketplaces concentrate trust, custody, and dispute resolution in a single intermediary that charges fees, can fail, and must be relied upon not to misbehave---costly and unnecessary for a campus community trading low-value items. We ask whether a marketplace's core trust functions---value, listings, escrow, and reputation---can be enforced entirely by public smart contracts, making custody, settlement, and reputation auditable while removing the single point of failure~\cite{nakamoto2008bitcoin,buterin2014ethereum}. The pattern recurs (resale, freelance milestones, rental deposits) and is a clear fit for a public blockchain (Section~\ref{sec:why}). We present Enigma, engineered as four \emph{vertical slices}---(1)~Token + Wallet, (2)~Listings, (3)~Escrow and Ratings, and (4)~Reputation---each owned by one member and spanning a contract, a front-end module, and a test suite, composing into one deployed system.
