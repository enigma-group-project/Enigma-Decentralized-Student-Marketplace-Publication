\textbf{Why this project, and why blockchain.} Peer-to-peer commerce within a community---students trading goods---is a setting where participants are mutually distrusting, transactions are low-value and frequent, and a centralized intermediary imposes fees, custody risk, and opacity. We adopt the suitability criteria of W\"ust and Gervais~\cite{wust2018blockchain}: a blockchain is warranted when (i) state must be stored, (ii) multiple parties write, (iii) those writers do not mutually trust one another, (iv) no always-available trusted third party is desired, and (v) public verifiability is valuable. Enigma satisfies all five: balances, listings, escrow, and reputation are shared state written by many buyers and sellers who do not trust each other, with no escrow company in the loop, and where auditability of custody and reputation is the central feature.

\textbf{Compatibility and suitability metrics.} The design maps onto the properties that justify a \emph{public, permissionless} chain: \emph{disintermediation} (escrow and settlement are enforced by code, not a platform), \emph{trustlessness} (correctness follows from contract logic and consensus, not operator honesty), \emph{immutability and auditability} (every state transition emits an event on an append-only ledger), \emph{transparency} (anyone can verify balances and outcomes), \emph{availability} (no single point of failure), and \emph{interoperability} (ENGC is a standard ERC-20, usable by any wallet). Because credits follow EIP-20~\cite{eip20} and EIP-2612~\cite{eip2612}, the system composes with the wider Ethereum ecosystem rather than a walled garden.

\textbf{Why it is a usable use case, and how it stands out.} The same escrow-plus-reputation pattern underlies online resale, freelance milestones, and rental deposits; the student marketplace is a concrete, low-risk instantiation that we deploy and exercise on the public Sepolia testnet. What distinguishes Enigma among comparable projects is rigor: it is built \emph{exclusively} on audited standards, its security properties are explicit and enforced (Section~\ref{sec:arch}), and every claim is backed by reproducible unit, fuzz, invariant, and static-analysis evidence (Sections~\ref{sec:metrics}--\ref{sec:evidence}).
