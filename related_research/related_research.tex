Decentralized commerce predates Enigma: peer-to-peer marketplaces such as OpenBazaar showed that listings and payments can operate without a central operator, and Ethereum~\cite{buterin2014ethereum} made programmable escrow and tokens practical. Fungible value is standardized by EIP-20~\cite{eip20}, with EIP-2612~\cite{eip2612} adding signature-based approvals that remove a transaction from the payment path. Production-grade security is typically inherited from audited component libraries rather than re-implemented; OpenZeppelin~\cite{openzeppelin} is the de-facto standard for access control, safe transfers, reentrancy guards, and pausability.

A parallel literature documents how these systems fail. Atzei \emph{et al.}~\cite{atzei2017survey} catalogue Ethereum attack classes---reentrancy, mishandled exceptions, and transaction-ordering dependence---that motivate the checks-effects-interactions discipline and guards Enigma adopts. Tooling has matured accordingly: Slither~\cite{feist2019slither} statically detects many of these patterns, and Foundry~\cite{foundry} enables unit, fuzz, and invariant testing. Our contribution is not a new primitive but a \emph{teaching-oriented, fully on-chain} marketplace that composes token, escrow, and reputation into one reproducibly tested system, partitioned into independently owned vertical slices.
